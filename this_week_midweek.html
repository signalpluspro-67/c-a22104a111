The Pullback Everyone Wanted Is Finally Here

Wednesday, October 14 2026

The Saturday thesis is still on track. Nothing in the tape since then broke it, and the weakness on Tuesday and Wednesday is the kind of giveback that strengthens a setup instead of killing it.

Let me tell you what the last three sessions actually did.

Saturday I wrote that this market had gotten too comfortable. VIX had been pinned near 14, credit spreads were tight, and the only thing anyone wanted to buy was size. The thesis was simple: when positioning gets that one sided, the market stops rewarding patience and starts punishing it. You don't need a catalyst. You need a crowd.

Then Monday came and went sideways. Tuesday opened soft and closed softer. Wednesday gave us the real move. S&P 769.37, off 1.1%. Nasdaq 730.49, down 1.8%. Small caps 271.35, off 1.9%. And VIX at 16.45, up 7.7% in a single session.

That is not a crash. That is a release valve.

Here is the part that matters. Look at where the selling showed up. IWM and QQQ led the downside, both roughly 1.9% and 1.8%, while the S&P held to 1.1%. That is a rotation signature, not a liquidation signature. When small caps and high-beta tech take the hit and the broad index only gives back half as much, you're watching money move, not money leave.

The VIX tells the same story. 16.45 sounds scary if you remember it at 12. It is not scary at all. We are still below the long run average. A 7.7% pop in the vol index on a day the S&P dropped 1.1% is a normal convexity response, not a regime change. Panic doesn't look like this. Panic looks like VIX north of 25 with the S&P down 3% and breadth collapsing.

So the thesis holds. The market is repricing comfort, and it's doing it in an orderly way.

The through line: the setups we flagged Saturday are now closer to their entries, not further away. Weakness is doing the work for you.
