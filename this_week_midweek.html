Semis Are Carrying a Load the Rest of the Tape Hasn't Agreed to Yet

Wednesday, October 21 2026

The Saturday thesis is still on track, but it's leaning on one leg now. The broad market rose again since the weekend, and the leadership got narrower while it did. That's the part you have to respect.

Monday was quiet. Tuesday gave the thrust. QQQ at 727.37, up 1.4% on the day, versus SPY at 773.82, up 0.6%, and IWM at 276.92, up 0.4%. That gap is the whole story. When the index that owns the megacaps runs at more than double the broad tape and three times the small caps, you're watching a handful of names do all the lifting. The VIX slipped to 16.74, down 3.3%, and a falling fear gauge next to narrowing breadth is the tape telling you it feels safe while fewer stocks actually participate. Both of those can be true. They just don't stay true together for long.

Here's the concrete tell. QQQ's move since Saturday came mostly from the semiconductor complex, and semis have a history of pulling the whole tape up behind them or leaving it stranded. If the rest of the market catches the bid into Friday, breadth expands and this is a clean continuation. If semis stall, the index that led gives back faster than the index that followed. Small caps tell you which one you're in. IWM sitting at 276.92 and barely moving while QQQ sprints is the yellow flag. Watch whether IWM can take out its recent high or not.

Our desk setups all sit on the same side of that question. The long scan threw up three names with matching confluence: a moving-average cross, a bottom pattern, and a pullback entry. If that cluster works while tech leads, you get the broad confirmation the tape hasn't given yet. Three longs triggering together in non-tech sectors is a breadth signal dressed up as a stock list. Watch them as a group, not one by one.

The thesis holds. It holds thinner than it did Saturday. You don't need to act on the doubt. You need to act on the level.
