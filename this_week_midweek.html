The Squeeze Nobody Priced

Wednesday, November 11 2026

The Saturday thesis is still on track. One session does not settle a week, but Tuesday's tape bent exactly the way the recap said it would, and it bent hard.

Here is what changed since the weekend. The S&P closed at 760.20, up 1.4%. The Nasdaq ran 2.7% to 692.97. That is the growth-over-value skew the recap flagged, and the index is now pressing the top of the range we drew on Saturday. The VIX fell 8.8% to 17.75. Read that number plainly: fear got cheaper while price got richer. That combination is a green light for risk, and it tells you the market already believes the worst is behind it.

The tell is in the internals. Small caps, the honest part of the tape, added only 0.8% to 282.88. When the Nasdaq outruns the Russell by roughly 2 to 1, the buying is concentrated, not broad. Money is chasing the biggest, most liquid growth names and leaving the rest behind. That is fine while it lasts. It also means the rally is top-heavy, and top-heavy rallies wobble when one or two leaders stumble.

This is where the desk setups earn their keep. Our three longs all lean into the same read: buy the names sitting at support with volume behind them, not the ones already extended.

AIG at 82.49 is the value side of the ledger. Post-earnings drift says the market underreacts to a good print, and this one is still climbing out of a base with our stop at 78.48 and a target at 88.51. It does not need a hot tape to work. It needs patience.

AVGO at 354.45 is the volume leader, a pullback off strength with a stop at 318.96 and a target at 407.69. This is the setup that benefits if Tuesday's Nasdaq bid keeps running. It is also the one that hurts most if the top-heavy rally cracks, because it lives in the crowded lane. Know which side of that you're on.

AME at 242.08 is the quiet one. A moving-average cross with a pullback entry, stop at 225.31, target at 267.24. Industrials do not need the Nasdaq to lead. If the tape broadens out and small caps catch up, this is the name that quietly grinds higher while the crowd watches megacap tech.

So the verdict holds: Saturday called for a market that rewards patience at support and punishes chasing extension, and Tuesday handed you exactly that. The growth squeeze is real. It is also narrow.

What I'm watching into Friday. First, the S&P at 760. If it holds that level on a closing basis, the risk-on read lives. A close back under 755 says Tuesday was a head-fake. Second, watch IWM against 285. Small caps reclaiming that line confirms the rally is widening, which flips AME from quiet setup to leader. If IWM stalls again, the tape stays a two-horse race between the index leaders and nothing else. Keep your stops where they are. Do not chase the 2.7%. Buy the pullbacks and let the squeeze come to you.
