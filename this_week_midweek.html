The Tape Keeps Drifting, and Nobody Cares

Wednesday, October 28 2026

Saturday I said this market was grinding higher on inertia, not conviction. Three days later, nothing has changed. The thesis is on track, maybe too perfectly.

SPY closed Tuesday at 771.26, up +0.2% on the day. QQQ at 722.42, same story. IWM at 280.33, barely breathing at +0.1%. The VIX slipped another 1% to 17.35. Every index moved in the same direction by the same tiny amount. That's not a market making decisions. That's a market waiting for permission.

The Quiet Part

When everything goes up the same amount on the same day, you're watching passive flows do their thing. There's no sector rotation. No risk-on, risk-off signal. No one is leaning anywhere. The bid is there because the bid has been there, and removing it would require a reason nobody has yet.

I've seen this pattern before. The VIX sitting in the high teens, not quite complacent, not quite worried. Realized vol compressing underneath. Daily ranges getting tighter. It looks calm. It is calm. But calm is a condition, not a forecast.

The question from Saturday was whether this grind could hold into month-end. So far the answer is yes, with an asterisk. The asterisk is volume. I want to see if Wednesday and Thursday bring any real participation or if we're coasting into the weekend on fumes.

Here's what caught my eye from the desk setups.

UAL at $104.52 is the one I keep coming back to. The setup tags are unusual activity, bottom formation, and a pullback into a level where buyers showed up before. Airlines have been messy all year, but UAL specifically has been building a base while the sector chops around. The target at $118.92 gives you a $14.40 upside against a $9.61 stop, so the reward-to-risk is roughly 1.5:1. That's not screaming, but with the confluence of signals it's worth a position. The key: if the broad market keeps grinding, names with bottom formations and pullback entries tend to be the ones that catch a bid when passive money rotates down the cap spectrum.

CX at $10.54 is interesting for a different reason. PEAD means post-earnings announcement drift, which tells you the stock reported and the market hasn't finished pricing the information. When you combine that with a bottom and a pullback, you're looking at a name where the fundamental catalyst and the technical setup agree. Target $11.44, stop $9.95. Tight. The risk is $0.59 for $0.90 of upside. In a low-vol tape like this, tight setups with a catalyst edge are how you extract returns without taking on directional market risk you can't control.

HTO rounds it out at $61.56 with a moving average crossover confirming the pullback and bottom signals. Target $66.19, stop $58.47. The stop is wider here, about $3.09 down versus $4.63 up, so the math works but you need to size accordingly.

Three longs. No shorts on the desk right now. That tells you something about the tape. When the systematic signals all point one direction, it usually means the path of least resistance is still higher, even if conviction is low. The absence of short setups is itself a signal. Sellers aren't organized.

But I want to be precise about what "still higher" means. This isn't a breakout environment. SPY has been adding fractions of a percent per day. If you're positioned for a melt-up, you'll be disappointed. If you're positioned for individual names to work within a stable backdrop, this is your window.

The philosophical frame here is simple. Markets don't need a reason to go up. They need a reason to stop going up. Right now I don't see the reason. That doesn't make me bullish. It makes me patient. I hold the setups that meet the criteria, I respect the stops, and I let the tape tell me when the regime changes.

Patience is a position. Boredom is information. When nothing is happening, the thing that's happening is continuation.

Into Friday

Two things I'm watching. First, SPY 771. That's the round number gravitational pull. If we close above it Wednesday and Thursday, month-end flows will likely carry us into November without drama. If we lose it intraday and can't reclaim by the close, that's the first crack in the grind.

Second, VIX 17. We're at 17.35 and drifting lower. A break below 17 would signal that options markets have fully priced out near-term risk. That's usually when a small vol expansion surprises people. I'm not predicting it. I'm noting that the setup for a surprise is forming, even as the surface stays flat.

Hold the names that are working. Size for the tape you have, not the tape you want. And if Friday rolls around and nothing has happened, that's fine. Sometimes the best midweek update is: the plan is still the plan.
