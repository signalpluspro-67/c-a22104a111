🗓️ Midweek Update

The Saturday thesis is wavering, not dead. The tape has drifted lower across the board, and the selling is broad enough to respect, but nothing has broken structurally.

Wednesday, September 23 2026

Small caps are the tell. IWM fell 1.8% on Tuesday to 282.08, the sharpest drop of the three major ETFs. That is not a tech-led air pocket. That is risk appetite leaving the building. When the smallest, most rate-sensitive names bleed faster than the megacaps, you're watching a market that is de-risking, not rotating. SPY at 766.91 is down 0.8%, QQQ at 738.59 is down 1.2%. The pattern is simple: the smaller the company, the harder the hit. That is a risk-off signature.

The VIX at 15.01, up 5.6% on the day, tells the same story. It's still below the stress threshold of 20, so this is a flinch, not a panic. But a 5.6% single-day jump in the fear gauge while the S&P only drops 0.8% means options traders are paying up for protection faster than the index is falling. That is a warning, not a headline.

Now the concrete anchor. Our desk's long setups from Saturday were AZZ at 138.95, LECO at 266.09, and MLI at 60.73. All three are industrial or industrial-adjacent names. All three had confluence from our internal signals: MA_CROSS, PULLBACK, BOTTOM. The thesis was that money would rotate into high-quality, cash-generative manufacturers with real earnings, not speculative narratives. The move since Saturday has not validated that rotation. It has paused it.

Look at the tape. These setups have not triggered cleanly. If you bought AZZ at 138.95 on Monday, you're sitting on a small loss against a stop at 131.66. That's a 5.2% buffer, and it's holding. LECO at 266.09 with a stop at 251.26 gives you 5.6% of room. MLI at 60.73 with a stop at 57.78 offers 4.9%. None of the stops have been hit. None of the targets have been reached. The trades are in the gray zone: not working, not broken.

That is the honest read. The Saturday thesis said "buy industrial strength on dip." The tape has not given you a dip to buy. It has given you a slow leak. The difference matters. A dip holds a level and bounces. A leak grinds lower without triggering the stop, testing your patience and your conviction. Right now, we are in the leak phase.

What would invalidate the thesis? A close below the stop levels on any of the three names. If IWM breaks below 275 on a closing basis, that likely drags MLI and AZZ with it. That is the line in the sand. If you're holding these, you do not need to guess. You need to respect the stop. The stop is not a suggestion. It is the price at which your reasoning was wrong.

Here's the counter-case. The 15.01 VIX is still low. The -0.8% SPY drop is within the normal noise of a bull market. Nothing about Tuesday's action screams distribution. The sellers are present, but they are not aggressive. Volume has not spiked to panic levels. This could be a two-day shakeout that resets sentiment, then resumes the grind higher. It has happened before. It will happen again.

The difference between a trader and a spectator is what you do with the ambiguity. You don't add to a position that's leaking. You don't abandon a position that's still above its stop. You hold, you tighten your focus, and you let the price tell you who's right.

What I'm watching into Friday. First, the 282 level on IWM. If small caps close below 278 by Thursday, that is a confirmed risk-off signal, and I would expect the industrial longs to follow. Second, the VIX at 16. A close above 16 on rising volume would tell me the protection buyers are winning, and I would trim half of any open long position into strength, not wait for the stop.

The setups are still valid. The market is just testing whether you believe them. The stops are the answer. Respect them, and the thesis will tell you when it's wrong. Ignore them, and the account will tell you for you.
