The Big Picture

Healthcare is exhausted. The sector sits in a late-chase phase where most of the money has already been made and new buyers are arriving after the move. That is a hostile backdrop for most medical device stocks.

The structural story in medical devices still favors companies shrinking the cost and footprint of diagnostics. Handheld ultrasound is one of the clearest expressions of that shift. The technology replaces cart-based systems that cost tens of thousands of dollars with a single probe connected to a phone or tablet. The total addressable market expands when the hardware gets cheap and portable enough for primary care, emergency response, and global health deployments.

A sector in late-chase mode punishes late entries and rewards pullbacks into intact trends. You want the stock that already ran, pulled back to support, and kept its long-term slope. That is exactly what the desk surfaced here.

Introducing BFLY

Butterfly Network designs and sells handheld ultrasound probes that plug into a smartphone or tablet. The core product line, Butterfly iQ+ and the newer iQ3, performs whole-body imaging from a single silicon-based chip-on-a-tip probe. The company sells direct in the United States and through distributors internationally.

The edge is the chip technology. Traditional ultrasound machines use piezoelectric crystals arranged in arrays, which makes the probes expensive and bulky. Butterfly replaced that architecture with a single silicon chip that can steer sound waves electronically. That cuts manufacturing cost and shrinks the probe to something that fits in a pocket. The software side matters too. Compass AI connects devices across hospital departments and ScanLab provides guided educational scanning for new users.

The company also sells Butterfly Move, which combines the portability of the handheld with the stability of a cart system, and a veterinary version of the iQ+ and iQ3. The product portfolio covers human medicine, bladder scanning, and animal care.
