Introducing BILL

BILL Holdings provides a cloud-based platform that automates accounts payable, accounts receivable, and spend management for small and midsize businesses. Think of it as the financial nervous system for a company with fifty to five hundred employees. It connects suppliers with clients, eliminates expense reports, and replaces manual cash flow management with an AI-enabled software layer.

The company changed its name from Bill.com Holdings in February 2023. It partners with accounting firms, banks, and software providers to distribute the platform. The revenue model is software-as-a-service plus transaction fees on payments volume. That dual engine matters. You get subscription recurring revenue and a payment take rate that scales with client activity.

Gross margins sit at 83.9%. That is the hallmark of a true platform business. The heavy lifting happens in code, not in physical fulfillment. The forward price-to-sales ratio is 2.8, and the forward price-to-earnings multiple is 10.9. Those are not nosebleed multiples for a +13.8% revenue grower with an 83.9% gross margin profile.

Risk Factors

The most direct risk is the drift fading. Post-earnings drift is a windowed edge. As time passes, the drift signal decays and the setup converts from a probabilistic drift trade to a binary event risk. If a macro print or a competitor's earnings lands inside your hold window, the edge evaporates. You cap your hold time and you commit to the stop.

Software valuations reprice violently on rate scares. The forward P/E of 10.9 is not stretched, but it does not protect you if the 10-year yield spikes and the whole sector de-rates by 20%. Long calls lose on both price and implied volatility in that scenario. You are not buying insurance. You are buying a technical setup with a known exit.

The quick ratio of 0.56 is a structural vulnerability. If payment float tightens or credit lines get renegotiated, the working capital gap bites. That is not a quarter-end risk, but it is the thing that can convert a 10% drawdown into a 20% gap down if it coincides with a macro event.

The single invalidation is a close below $39.98. That is the stop. That level sits below the swing low of $31.96 adjusted for the $2.21 ATR. A break there says the drift has failed. You exit. No averaging down.

Timing risk around the catalyst is nil because there is no hard catalyst. That cuts both ways. No event means no binary gap risk. But it also means no forced repricing moment. You need patience and you need to let the drift work.
