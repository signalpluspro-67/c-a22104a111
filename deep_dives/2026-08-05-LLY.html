The Big Picture

Healthcare sits in a strange spot right now. The sector is NEUTRAL overall, but the acceleration signal just flipped to NEW_DOWN. That tells you money is starting to leave the group at the margin, even if the floor has not broken.

You would think a sector rolling over would be a reason to avoid every stock in it. You'd be wrong. When a sector decelerates, capital concentrates. Money does not leave healthcare entirely. It leaves the laggards and piles into the compounders that still grow regardless of the macro headline.

The structural force here is the GLP-1 therapeutic class. These drugs treat type 2 diabetes and obesity, and the addressable market is measured in the hundreds of millions of patients worldwide. That demand curve is independent of the business cycle. People do not stop treating diabetes because GDP softens.

This backdrop rewards a name with accelerating earnings, a rising long-term trend, and enough size to absorb capital rotating out of weaker healthcare stocks. Eli Lilly checks every one of those boxes.

Introducing LLY

Eli Lilly is a 1876-founded pharmaceutical company headquartered in Indianapolis with roughly 50,000 employees. The company discovers, develops, manufactures, and markets human pharmaceutical products across the United States, Europe, China, Japan, and other international markets.

The portfolio spans four main areas. Cardiometabolic health is the crown jewel, led by Mounjaro for type 2 diabetes and Zepbound for obesity, alongside a deep insulin franchise including Humalog, Humulin, and Basaglar. The oncology business includes Verzenio for breast cancer, Retevmo for metastatic NSCLC, Cyramza for gastric cancer, and Erbitux for colorectal and head and neck cancers. Immunology covers Taltz for plaque psoriasis, Ebglyss for severe atopic dermatitis, Olumiant for rheumatoid arthritis, and Omvoh for ulcerative colitis. Neuroscience rounds it out with Emgality for migraine prevention and Kisubla for symptomatic Alzheimer's disease.

The edge is clear. Mounjaro and Zepbound sit at the center of the largest therapeutic expansion in a generation. The company has collaborations with Boehringer Ingelheim for Jardiance, with Roche and Genentech for lebrikizumab, license agreements with Almirall for Ebglyss and Chugai for orforglipron, and strategic partnerships with Ascidian Therapeutics and BioArctic. The pipeline is deep and the commercial machine is already built.
