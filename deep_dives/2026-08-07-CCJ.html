The Big Picture

Uranium is a structural story that has not played out yet. The world is restarting reactors, extending licenses, and signing long-term contracts at prices well above the cost of marginal production. The supply side is thin. Years of underinvestment after Fukushima left the project pipeline empty. Kazatomprom has flagged production shortfalls. Secondary supplies from inventory drawdowns are fading. That sets up a multi-year window where contracted volumes have to be replaced at higher prices.

The demand side is accelerating. China has 30+ reactors under construction and plans to keep building. Europe is extending plant life. Even the United States is keeping nuclear in the mix. Small modular reactor development is early but it adds optionality. The net result is a commodity where physical tightness meets policy support. That is rare.

This backdrop rewards producers with Tier One assets and contracted books that reprice higher over time. It also rewards patience. Uranium equities have been volatile. They ran hard in 2023 and 2024, then pulled back as spot stabilized. You are buying into a theme where the fundamentals are still tightening but the stocks have already had one round of euphoria and disappointment.

That is where the opportunity sits. The theme is intact. The stocks are cheaper. The option market is pricing low expectations.

Introducing CCJ

Cameco is the largest publicly traded uranium company outside of Kazakhstan. It mines, mills, and sells uranium concentrate. It refines and converts that concentrate into the forms utilities need for reactor fuel. It fabricates fuel assemblies. And through its Westinghouse stake, it designs reactors and services them. That is a full-cycle nuclear business, from the ground to the reactor core.

The company operates some of the highest-grade uranium mines in the world, in Saskatchewan's Athabasca Basin. McArthur River and Cigar Lake are Tier One assets with low cash costs when they are running. That gives it exposure to the installed base of reactors globally, a recurring revenue stream from outage services, engineering support, and component replacement.

Cameco sells most of its uranium under long-term contracts with utilities. That means realized prices lag the spot market. When spot runs higher, Cameco's contract book reprices gradually over years. That is the core investment dynamic. You are buying a producer whose earnings power is tied to a contract book that renews at progressively higher prices.
