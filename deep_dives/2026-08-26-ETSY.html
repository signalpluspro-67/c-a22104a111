The Big Picture

Consumer discretionary is fighting two headwinds. The rate cycle is higher for longer, and the post-covid rotation into services is still draining dollars from goods. Internet retail gets the worst of it. Most stocks in the sector are charting lower highs. The ones that held a bid are compressing at resistance. You have a pool of sub-$10 billion names that are either falling knives or dead money, waiting for a catalyst that may not come before quarter-end.

Etsy sits in the middle of that mess, but it is not crushed. It is clinging to the top of its yearly range while the broad market struggles. Introducing ETSY

Etsy operates two-sided marketplaces that connect artisans, vintage sellers, and resale fashion sellers with buyers. The main Etsy platform is the core, focused on handmade and unique goods. Depop is a smaller, fast-growing fashion resale marketplace popular with Gen Z. The company generates revenue from transaction fees, listing fees, advertising, and seller services like shipping labels and payment processing. It is a capital-light model with a 71.4% gross margin.

The business is a niche internet retailer, not a broad goods platform. That focus gives it some insulation from the Amazon/Walmart price war. Buyers come for specific items they cannot find elsewhere. The seller base is sticky because the marketplace is purpose-built for their inventory type. The risks are obvious: a discretionary spending slowdown hits artisanal goods harder than essentials, and a 1.82 beta means the stock amplifies market moves. But the balance sheet carries 12.23 per share in cash. The platform has survived and grown through one cycle already.
