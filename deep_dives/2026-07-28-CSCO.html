Introducing CSCO

Cisco Systems sells the plumbing of the internet. The company designs, develops, and sells the networking equipment that moves data through campuses, data centers, and branch offices worldwide. It builds the switches, routers, and wireless access points that connect everything. It also sells the security layer that protects those connections, the collaboration tools through Webex, and the observability software that monitors network health.

The business has shifted. Cisco spent years as a hardware vendor selling boxes with perpetual licenses. That model flattened out. The company pivoted to recurring revenue. Software subscriptions, cloud-delivered security, and services now drive an increasing share of the top line. That transition matters because recurring revenue compounds differently. It carries higher margins over time and creates a stickier customer base.

Cisco acquired Splunk, a massive data platform, to deepen its software and observability footprint. That deal expanded the addressable market. It brought Cisco deeper into the data pipeline. The company now sells network infrastructure plus the analytics layer that sits on top of it.
