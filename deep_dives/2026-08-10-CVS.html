Introducing CVS

CVS Health is a vertically integrated healthcare company operating three segments. Health Care Benefits runs Aetna, the insurance arm offering Medicare Advantage, Medicaid, pharmacy drug plans, and commercial coverage. Health Services runs Caremark, the pharmacy benefit manager handling formulary design, retail network management, specialty pharmacy, and clinical services for employers and health plans. Pharmacy and Consumer Wellness is the retail pharmacy footprint, with thousands of stores selling prescriptions, over-the-counter products, and general merchandise.

The vertical integration is the edge. A patient fills a prescription at a CVS store, the claim is processed through Caremark, and the insurance risk is held by Aetna. That structure captures revenue at multiple touchpoints in the healthcare dollar. Whether that structure captures enough margin at each touchpoint is the debate.

The company has spent years building this model through acquisition. Aetna was the transformative deal, bringing insurance in-house. The thesis was that controlling the insurance, the PBM, and the retail pharmacy would let CVS manage total cost of care better than any competitor. That thesis is still being tested. The margin profile tells you the integration is not yet producing the profitability the bull case promised.
