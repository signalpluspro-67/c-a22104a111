Introducing GE

GE Aerospace is what remains after years of corporate dismantling. The old General Electric conglomerate split itself into three separate companies. GE Aerospace is the aviation business, focused on designing and manufacturing jet engines for commercial and defense customers.

The company operates through two segments. Commercial Engines and Services handles jet engine manufacturing, spare parts sales, and maintenance and overhaul work for airlines and business aviation. Defense and Propulsion Technologies builds engines and systems for governments, militaries, and commercial airframers. That segment also covers avionics, power systems, small turboprop engines, and additive manufacturing through brands like Avio Aero, Dowty Propellers, and Colibrium Additive.

The edge is installed base. GE and its joint venture CFM International have a massive fleet of engines already flying. Every one of those engines generates recurring service revenue over a multi-decade lifecycle. The deeper the installed base grows, the more predictable the services revenue becomes. That is the moat.
