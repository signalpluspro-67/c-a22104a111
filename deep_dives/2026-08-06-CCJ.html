The Big Picture

Nuclear power is back in the policy conversation. The structural shift is real. Governments that spent two decades backing away from uranium are now extending reactor lifetimes, approving new builds, and signing long-term supply contracts. The Inflation Reduction Act brought production tax credits to existing nuclear plants in the United States. Europe is reconsidering its atomic exit. Asia continues to build reactors at scale. All of this feeds directly into uranium demand.

The supply side is tighter than it has been in years. Years of underinvestment in new mine capacity left a structural deficit. Producers held back output through the bear market. Utilities ran down inventories. Now those utilities need to lock in fuel for reactors that will run for decades. The contract price has been climbing. The spot price has been volatile but the direction is clear. This backdrop rewards companies with proven reserves, permitted mines, and the processing infrastructure to turn yellowcake into fuel.

You own uranium because the world needs baseload clean power and the fuel supply is constrained. The question is which name gives you the best exposure and at what cost. That is where this gets complicated.

Introducing CCJ

Cameco is the largest publicly traded uranium company in the world. It is headquartered in Saskatoon, Canada and was incorporated in 1987. The company operates in three segments. The Uranium segment handles exploration, mining, milling, and sale of uranium concentrate. The Fuel Services segment refines and converts that concentrate into usable fuel and sells conversion services. The Westinghouse segment is the newer addition. Cameco acquired a stake in Westinghouse, the nuclear reactor technology manufacturer and service provider. That gives Cameco exposure to reactor design, outage and maintenance services, instrumentation, and engineering support for commercial utilities and government agencies.

The Westinghouse stake matters because it changes the business mix. Cameco is no longer a pure miner. It is a vertically integrated nuclear fuel and services company. The uranium business gives you commodity exposure. The fuel services business gives you margin stability through the conversion and fabrication chain. The Westinghouse business gives you recurring service revenue tied to the global reactor fleet. That diversification is the edge.

Cameco sells its uranium and fuel products to nuclear utilities across the Americas, Europe, and Asia. The customer base is investment-grade utilities signing long-term contracts. That revenue visibility is a structural advantage over smaller producers who rely on spot sales.
