The Big Picture

Basic Materials is in a momentum phase. The sector is accelerating, and the internal read flags early entry conditions. That tells you the rotation is fresh. Most investors have not repositioned for it yet.

Steel sits at the center of this move. Infrastructure spending, reshoring of manufacturing, and tariff protections on imported steel have changed the supply picture for US producers. Domestic steel capacity is tight. When demand pulses, the leveraged producers expand margins fast.

This backdrop rewards the most cyclical, most US-exposed names first. A company with high beta, deep domestic operations, and a stock price still far from its highs gets the most torque from a sector turn. You want the name that moves twice as much as the sector when the cycle bends.

Introducing CLF

Cleveland-Cliffs is the largest flat-rolled steel producer in North America. The company runs vertically integrated operations from iron ore mining through steelmaking and downstream finishing. It serves automotive, infrastructure, manufacturing, and steel distributors. Headquartered in Cleveland, Ohio, the company employs roughly 25,000 people across the United States and Canada.

The edge is integration. Cleveland-Cliffs controls its own iron ore supply, produces pellets and direct reduced iron, and processes ferrous scrap through its own steelmaking and finishing operations. That vertical chain means less exposure to raw material cost spikes that crush margins at non-integrated mini-mills. When steel prices rise, the captured raw material savings flow straight to operating income.

The company also produces specialized grades including grain-oriented electrical steel, non-oriented electrical steel, and auto chrome. These are high-value products with thinner competition. The electrical steel line ties Cleveland-Cliffs directly to transformer and grid investment, which is a multi-year demand cycle independent of conventional construction steel. You get cyclical exposure to steel prices plus a structural hook into grid modernization.
