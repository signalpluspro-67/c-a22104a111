Introducing PCG

PG&E Corporation is the parent of Pacific Gas and Electric Company. It sells and delivers electricity and natural gas to millions of customers across northern and central California. Its generation fleet spans nuclear, hydroelectric, fossil fuel-fired, fuel cells, and solar. It owns the transmission lines, the substations, the distribution lines, and the gas pipeline network that serves its territory.

The company is a regulated utility. That means its returns are set by state regulators at the California Public Utilities Commission. It earns a allowed return on its invested capital. The business model is capital intensive. PG&E spends billions on infrastructure, then recovers those costs from ratepayers over time through approved rate cases.

The history is rocky. PG&E went through bankruptcy after the 2019 and 2020 wildfire seasons. It emerged restructured with a liability fund in place. The stock has been climbing back since. You are looking at a company that survived an existential crisis and is now trading at 92.8% of its 52-week high. The market is pricing in a story of rehabilitation.
