Introducing DAL

Delta Air Lines is the largest US carrier by revenue, headquartered in Atlanta with a network anchored on hubs in Detroit, Minneapolis-St. Paul, and Salt Lake City. The company runs roughly 1,314 aircraft and employs about 103,000 people. Its two segments are Airline and Refinery. The refinery piece, the Trainer facility in Pennsylvania, gives Delta a partial hedge on jet fuel costs that no other major US carrier replicates at the same scale.

The domestic network is the cash engine. The international network, anchored on Amsterdam, London-Heathrow, Paris-Charles de Gaulle, Seoul-Incheon, and Tokyo, carries the premium-cabin revenue and the corporate contracts. Delta's edge has always been hub dominance, loyalty program economics, and a revenue-management system that squeezes more per seat-mile than peers.

You also get exposure to vacation packages, maintenance and engineering services, and cargo. The loyalty program, SkyMiles, is a deferred-revenue liability on the balance sheet that converts to cash as members redeem miles. That is a structural advantage most investors price too cheaply.
