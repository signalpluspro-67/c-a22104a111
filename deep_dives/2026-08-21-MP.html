The Big Picture

Rare earth magnets are the hardest input for the West to replace, and capital has finally arrived to fix it. Neodymium-iron-boron magnets sit inside EV traction motors, wind turbines, industrial robots, drones and precision-guided munitions. China controls the refining and the sintering. That concentration stopped being a cost question and became a security question, and Western governments now budget for it like one.

The sector read is the complication. Basic Materials currently registers as exhausted, meaning the move has already run and the sector is stretched rather than early. The follow-through read is still riding, so the trend is intact and buyers keep showing up. You get a live theme and a late entry in the same package. That argues for defined risk and a real stop, not for size.

Critical minerals and rare earth elements are the two themes carrying this money. Both are policy themes as much as commodity themes. A higher oxide price does not fix a supply chain when the bottleneck is separation capacity and magnet-making capacity, and both take years to permit and commission. Money can accelerate that. It cannot skip it.

A backdrop like this pays the operator that already owns a permitted, producing asset and is building the downstream behind it. Ownership of the scarce physical link is the whole edge. Everyone else is holding a deposit and a timeline.

Introducing $MP

MP Materials operates Mountain Pass, the rare earth mine and processing facility in San Bernardino County, California, and the only asset of its kind at scale in the Western Hemisphere. The company runs two segments. Materials covers the mine, the concentrate and the separated rare earth oxides. Magnetics covers the step that matters most: NdPr metal and finished NdFeB permanent magnets.

That second segment is the reason this exists as an investment. Mining rare earths is the easy part. Separating them, converting oxide to metal, and then producing magnets to spec inside the United States is the part that has not existed commercially in decades. MP is building the full chain under one roof, from rock to magnet.

The company was founded in 2017, is headquartered in Las Vegas, and runs the whole operation with roughly 998 employees. What you're buying is execution on capacity that is still being built.

Why Now

The flow is the actual reason for the timing. That flow builds the call wall at 70, which pulls price toward it as dealers hedge, and it defends the put wall at 55 on the way down. You're trading inside a structure that other people's positioning created.

Exhausted plus riding means late in a live trend. Late trends pay, and they pay less than early ones, so the position should reflect that.

Risk Factors

The move has already happened and that's the primary risk. The stock added +34.5% in a month into an exhausted sector. Buying the fourth week of a 34.5% run means the easy money was made by somebody else, and mean reversion after a run like that is normal rather than exceptional.

The 30-week average falling at -3.6% is the second problem. Price above a declining long-term average is a recovery, and recoveries fail back into the average more often than uptrends do. Over 63 days the stock is down -2.7% and behind the S&P by -5.8%, which is the honest longer view of this chart.

The cash burn is the fundamental risk. If the downstream slips, the 24.3 price-to-sales multiple and the 64.6 forward P/E have no support underneath them. Beta at 1.92 guarantees a market drawdown hits this roughly twice as hard.

If you express this with calls, decay works against you every day with no dated event before early November to pay for the wait. That's the specific cost of a trade with no catalyst.

The invalidation is a close below 52.87. That level sits beneath the put wall at 55, so losing it means dealer support failed and the 55 to 70 structure is gone. That's -12.0% from 60.05, and it's the whole risk you signed up for.
