The Big Picture

Quantum computing sits in the gap between science experiment and commercial product. The broad market treats it as speculative. The capital flows tell you that governments and defense primes are committing real dollars to quantum sensing, secure networking, and navigation systems that work without GPS. The United States, Europe, and Asia are all funding national-security applications. That is where the money is right now.

The technology sector rewards companies that can convert research grants and defense contracts into recurring revenue. Most quantum names cannot. They burn cash, dilute shareholders, and promise breakthroughs that sit years away. The ones that survive long enough to matter are the ones with enough balance-sheet cushion to reach commercial scale.

This backdrop rewards a name with a clean balance sheet and exposure to government and defense spending. It also rewards anyone willing to buy cheap optionality before the market cares again. You are not investing in quantum computing today. You are buying a cheap ticket and waiting to see if the sector catches a bid.

Introducing INFQ

Infleqtion, Inc. is a quantum technology company headquartered in Louisville, Colorado. It was founded in 2007 and formerly operated as ColdQuanta before changing its name in January 2026. The company builds neutral atom solutions for quantum computing, networking, sensing, and security. Its product portfolio spans quantum clocks, RF sensors, inertial and gravimetric sensors for GPS-denied environments, quantum cores, and quantum software.

The company serves organizations across national security, critical infrastructure, space, defense, energy, finance, and telecommunications. Its customer base leans heavily toward government and defense applications. That is the durable part of the business. Governments fund quantum research because they need secure communications and navigation systems that function without satellite signals.

Infleqtion employs 203 people. The gap between valuation and revenue is extreme. You are paying for optionality on a sector that has not yet matured. The company's edge is its neutral atom technology platform, which applies across computing, sensing, and networking. Whether that edge converts into commercial traction is the open question.
