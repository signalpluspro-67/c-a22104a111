Introducing COOK

Traeger built the wood pellet grill category. The company designs, sources, and sells internet-connected pellet grills, flat top grills, wood pellets, rubs, sauces, and accessories under the Traeger brand. It runs a content ecosystem through its app and the Traegerhood community, pairing hardware with recipes, videos, and lifestyle content to drive engagement and repeat purchases. The connected-device angle means every grill sold is a pipeline for recurring pellet, accessory, and content revenue.

The business has 433 employees and trades at a market cap of $178M. That is small. Headquartered in Salt Lake City, Traeger went public during the SPAC-era consumer boom and has since round-tripped from the highs. The stock bottomed near $20.91 and has been rebuilding since. You are looking at a company where the equity story is about stabilization after a brutal drawdown, not growth at any cost.

The product line is sticky. Grills last for years, and pellet consumption creates a consumable revenue stream that compounds with the installed base. Accessories, sauces, and rubs layer on top. The MEATER smart thermometer extends the connected ecosystem. The question is whether Traeger can stop shrinking revenue and convert that installed base into enough recurring cash to service its debt load.
