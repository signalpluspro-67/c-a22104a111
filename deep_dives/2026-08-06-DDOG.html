The Big Picture

Technology is in a momentum regime with early acceleration. That means the broad market is rewarding growth names again, and the initial push has room to run.

The structural shift underneath is cloud adoption. Enterprises keep moving workloads to AWS, Azure, and GCP. That migration creates a monitoring problem. You cannot manage what you cannot see. Observability platforms sit directly in the path of that spend.

The second force is AI. Every new large language model deployment needs performance tracking, cost visibility, and security. Datadog built specific products for that: LLM observability, bits AI for site reliability engineering, and cloud cost management. The demand loop is tightening.

The founder-led theme matters here. Founder-led software companies tend to reinvest aggressively during growth windows. They prioritize product expansion over short-term margin optics. This backdrop rewards a company with that posture and a product set tied to secular cloud tailwinds.

Introducing DDOG

Datadog is a cloud observability and security platform. It serves developers and operations teams who need to monitor infrastructure, application performance, logs, and network traffic in real time.

The product surface is wide. Infrastructure monitoring, application performance monitoring, log management, database monitoring, network monitoring, synthetics, real user monitoring, and incident response all sit inside one platform. The company added LLM observability, cloud security, code security, and cloud SIEM as newer layers. That breadth means once a customer integrates Datadog, the switching cost is enormous. Rip and replace is painful. Expansion within the installed base drives revenue growth.

Datadog sells on a consumption basis. Customers pay for what they use. When workloads grow, revenue grows. When new products get adopted, average revenue per customer expands. The model compounds. The company employs roughly 8,100 people and is headquartered in New York. It was incorporated in 2010.
