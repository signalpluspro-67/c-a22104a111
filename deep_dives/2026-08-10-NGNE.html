The Big Picture

The structural shift underneath is real. Genetic medicine is moving from promise to clinical proof. It rewards companies with actual patients enrolled and actual data on the calendar.

This backdrop punishes the speculative and rewards the tested. You want a name that already ran, pulled back on low volume, and kept its trend intact. That is exactly what the desk surfaced in Neurogene.

Introducing NGNE

Neurogene is a clinical-stage biotechnology company based in New York with 131 employees. It develops genetic medicines for rare neurological diseases. The company was founded in 2018.

The lead asset is NGN-401, an adeno-associated virus 9 vector in Phase 1/2 trials for Rett syndrome. Rett is a severe, rare neurological disorder with no approved disease-modifying therapy. The second asset is NGN-101, a gene therapy candidate in Phase 1/2 for CLN5 Batten disease, another devastating rare pediatric condition.

The pipeline is narrow and focused. Two programs, both in early clinical testing, both targeting diseases with profound unmet need. Neurogene holds license agreements with The University of Edinburgh, Virovek, Sigma-Aldrich, and Stanford. This is a binary story. Clinical data drives the stock, and everything else is noise.
