Introducing $DAL

Delta Air Lines is the largest US carrier by revenue and the most financially disciplined name in the airline industry. It runs a hub-and-spoke network anchored in Atlanta, Detroit, Minneapolis-St. Paul, and Salt Lake City, with coastal positions in Boston, Los Angeles, New York, and Seattle. Its international network stretches across Amsterdam, London-Heathrow, Paris, Seoul-Incheon, and Tokyo. The company also owns a refinery subsidiary, which gives it a partial hedge against jet-fuel cost swings.

Delta's edge is its loyalty program and its premium revenue mix. It has spent years pushing customers up the fare ladder into premium cabins and loyalty-driven bookings. That mix produces better margins than the legacy peers who still depend heavily on basic economy fares. The Skymiles program drives repeat bookings and gives Delta a moat that discount carriers cannot replicate.

The fleet runs roughly 1,314 aircraft. The company employs 103,000 people. It is headquartered in Atlanta and was founded in 1924. You are buying a mature, scaled operator with a proven ability to generate cash through cycles. It is not a growth story. It is a free-cash-flow story trading at a compressed multiple inside a sector that is leading.
