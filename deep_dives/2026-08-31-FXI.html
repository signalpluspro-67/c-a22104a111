The Big Picture

China is the trade everyone wants to call and nobody wants to hold. The valuations are low. The policy backdrop is shifting. The access is messy. For a US investor, the cleanest way into the large-cap Chinese equity market is the iShares China Large-Cap ETF, FXI.

The fund tracks the biggest Chinese companies that list in Hong Kong and are open to international buyers. That means Alibaba, Tencent, Meituan, the state banks, the insurers. The index excludes China A-shares, the mainland listings that foreign money can only touch through quotas and swap structures. What you get with FXI is the institutional, liquid, internationally tradable slice of China.

That slice has been cheap for a while. The fund trades at a trailing price-to-earnings multiple of 9.6. The broad US market rarely gets that low outside a recession. China has had its own recession scare already, plus a property collapse, plus a government crackdown cycle, plus an export slowdown. The market has priced in a lot of bad news.

That is the backdrop. A large, liquid, cheap equity market with a long list of known problems and a policy apparatus that can turn on a dime. If Beijing decides to stimulate, or if sentiment simply snaps back, a fund like this can move fast. The risk is that it does not move at all. This trade is a low-conviction lottery ticket on the chance that it does.

Introducing FXI

FXI is the iShares China Large-Cap ETF. It holds the largest Chinese companies that trade on the Hong Kong Stock Exchange. The fund is non-diversified, which under US fund rules means it can concentrate heavily in its biggest positions. In practice, the top handful of internet and financial companies dominate the index.

The fund commits at least 80% of its assets to the component securities of its underlying index. The rest can go into economically similar instruments. This is a standard index-tracking structure. You are not buying an active manager's view on China. You are buying a rules-based basket of the biggest, most liquid Chinese names available to international investors.

What FXI offers is pure beta. It is the broad, large-cap Chinese equity market in one ticker, with deep volume and tight spreads. It is the vehicle of choice for institutions that want China exposure without setting up a Hong Kong account or dealing with individual single-stock risk. That liquidity matters for an options trade with a two-and-a-half-week shelf life.

Risk Factors

That is the expected outcome on any lottery pick, and the desk says it plainly. There is no tested edge behind this trade.

The timing risk is acute. Two and a half weeks is a short window. Even a genuine catalyst can arrive after expiry and leave you with nothing. China policy moves have their own calendar, and there is no guarantee any of it lands inside this window.
