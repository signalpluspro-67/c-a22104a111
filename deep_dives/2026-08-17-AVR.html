Introducing AVR

Anteris Technologies Global Corp. makes one thing that matters: the DurAVR transcatheter heart valve. It is a tiny crown of tissue and wire, a single piece designed to replace a failing aortic valve without cracking the chest. What sets it apart is the shape. The company built it to mimic the natural performance of a healthy human aortic valve, a claim most competitors cannot make.

The valve sits on a foundation of their ADAPT anti-calcification tissue treatment and deploys via their ComASUR steerable delivery system. This is not a platform company with five pipeline assets. It is a focused bet on aortic stenosis, a disease that kills in predictable numbers every year. The engine exists. The scale does not.

Anteris is based in Australia, listed in the US, with 174 employees and a history of burning cash to get DurAVR through trials and toward approval. No dividend, no buybacks. Every dollar is reinvested into that single device.
