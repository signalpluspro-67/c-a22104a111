The Big Picture

Healthcare has been quietly out of favor. The sector spent the last two years behind technology and energy while rates stayed high and biotech funding contracted. That created a generation of beaten-down clinical-stage companies trading near cash. The survivors are the ones that kept their pipelines alive through the drought.

Gene and cell therapy is the structural shift inside healthcare that still matters. The science has advanced. The regulatory pathway has clarified. What has not returned is the willingness of the broad market to pay for pre-revenue biotech. That sets up a specific opportunity. You get companies with real platforms trading at fractions of where they would sit in a risk-on cycle.

This backdrop rewards a specific kind of name. You want a company whose stock has already begun to recover, whose option market is pricing zero expectations, and whose downside is already largely discounted. The combination is rare. When you find it, the asymmetric payoff is real, but so is the probability of a total loss.

Introducing PGEN

Precigen is a clinical-stage biopharmaceutical company headquartered in Germantown, Maryland. It develops gene and cell therapies using precision technology across three areas: immuno-oncology, autoimmune disorders, and infectious diseases. The company employs 160 people.

The core platforms are UltraCAR-T and AdenoVerse. UltraCAR-T delivers chimeric antigen receptor T-cell therapies for cancer patients. AdenoVerse uses a library of proprietary adenovectors for gene delivery of therapeutic effectors, immunomodulators, and vaccine antigens. The pipeline includes PRGN-3005 in a Phase 1/1b trial for advanced ovarian cancer, PRGN-3006 in a Phase 1b trial for relapsed acute myeloid leukemia, and PRGN-2009 in a Phase 2 trial for HPV-positive solid tumors.

Precigen was formerly known as Intrexon Corporation. It changed its name in February 2020. The company was founded in 1998. It also sells the UltraPorator, a proprietary electroporation device used in cell therapy manufacturing. The platform approach is what differentiates Precigen from single-asset biotechs. Multiple programs can read out across different indications, which means more shots on goal from the same technology base.
